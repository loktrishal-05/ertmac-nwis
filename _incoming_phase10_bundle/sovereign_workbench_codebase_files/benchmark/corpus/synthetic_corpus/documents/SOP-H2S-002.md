## 3. Response to a personal H2S alarm or confirmed H2S release

A personal monitor alarm is treated as a real exposure event. It is not treated as a suspected
instrument fault, and the response below is not delayed for any reason.

**Required actions, in this order**

1. **Withdraw immediately.** Leave the area at once. Do not stop to investigate the source,
   do not stop to take a further reading, and do not wait to see whether the alarm clears.
2. **Move upwind.** Move upwind of the area and continue to the designated muster or safe
   location for that area.
3. **Notify the control room.** Report the alarm, the location and the time to the control
   room as soon as it is safe to speak. Notification is made even if the alarm has stopped.
4. **Follow the site emergency procedure.** The emergency procedure governs from this point,
   including area control, rescue and any re-entry decision.

**Additional requirements**

- Re-entry to the area is made only under the site emergency procedure, with appropriate
  respiratory protection and under the authority of the incident controller.
- The absence of symptoms does not indicate a safe atmosphere. H2S deadens the sense of smell
  at higher concentrations, and a person may feel unaffected while exposed. A worker who feels
  well after an alarm still withdraws, still moves upwind and is still assessed.
- Every alarm is recorded and investigated, including an alarm that stops on its own.

**What this section does not permit**

There is no concentration, duration or circumstance under which this procedure permits a
worker to remain in the area after a personal alarm in order to obtain a further measurement,
to confirm the reading, or to complete a task in progress. There is no exception for a
short-duration task. This procedure does not authorize any reader or automated system to
direct a worker to remain in, or return to, an area where an H2S alarm has occurred.
