## 4. Response to abnormal exchanger performance

This section applies when exchanger performance departs from the recent operating pattern —
for example a rising differential pressure, a reducing thermal duty, or an outlet temperature
drifting away from its usual value.

**Step 1 — Verify the measurements before interpreting them**

1. Verify inlet and outlet temperatures on both sides against the control system and, where
   fitted, the local indicators.
2. Verify shell-side and tube-side pressures and the derived differential pressure.
3. Verify the flow measurement. A differential pressure is interpreted only together with the
   flow through the exchanger at the same time. A differential pressure that has risen while
   flow has also risen has not, on that evidence alone, demonstrated a change in exchanger
   condition.
4. Confirm the instruments are in service and that none is in a held, forced or failed state.

**Step 2 — Check fouling indicators**

Fouling is assessed from the pattern of several indicators together, not from any single one:

- differential pressure across the exchanger, considered against flow;
- the approach temperature and its trend since the last cleaning;
- thermal duty compared with the period following the last recorded cleaning;
- the maintenance history of the exchanger, including the date and result of the last cleaning.

A single elevated reading is an observation. Fouling is a hypothesis, and it remains a
hypothesis until it is confirmed by inspection. This procedure does not treat a differential
pressure rise as confirmation of fouling.

**Step 3 — Alternative explanations**

Before fouling is proposed as the explanation, the following are considered and recorded:

- an increase in throughput or flow through the exchanger;
- a change in feed composition, viscosity or temperature;
- a change in the upstream or downstream line-up, including bypass position;
- instrument drift or a failed transmitter.

**Step 4 — Authorization for any process change**

Any change to exchanger operation — including a bypass valve position change, a flow set-point
change, a temperature controller set-point change, or taking the exchanger out of service —
requires the authorization of the Console Operator and the Shift Supervisor.

A performance observation, however clear, is not authorization. This procedure does not
authorize any reader or automated system to make, command or approve a process change. Any
proposal arising from this section is a proposal for review by those roles.

**Step 5 — Recording**

Record the observations, the verification performed, the alternative explanations considered,
and any proposal raised for review.
