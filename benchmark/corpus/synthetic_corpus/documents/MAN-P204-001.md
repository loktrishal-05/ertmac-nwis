## 3. Vibration guidance and inspection

### 3.1 Normal running vibration

Under normal running conditions this pump operates with an overall vibration velocity
**below 4.5 mm/s RMS**, measured at the drive-end bearing housing.

The 4.5 mm/s figure is the vendor's expectation for a healthy machine in good condition. It is
a guide to normal running only. **It is not an alarm limit, not an alert limit and not a trip
setting.** Alert and shutdown limits for this pump are set by the site and are given in the
site operating procedure for the equipment, not in this manual.

### 3.2 Inspection checks for elevated vibration

Where vibration is elevated above the normal running range, the following are inspected. The
list is a diagnostic starting point. **Each item is a check to be performed, not a finding, and
none of these items is a cause until inspection establishes it as one.**

1. **Alignment.** Check shaft alignment between pump and driver, including soft foot. Record
   the measured alignment against the last recorded alignment.
2. **Bearings.** Check bearing condition, lubrication level and lubricant condition. Check the
   bearing housing temperature against its usual running value. Take a lubricant sample where
   the condition is in doubt.
3. **Cavitation indicators.** Check for indications associated with cavitation, including
   available suction pressure against the required value, suction strainer condition, suction
   line restriction, and any change in the noise character of the machine.
4. **Foundation.** Check the foundation, baseplate, grout condition and holding-down bolt
   torque. Check for any looseness at the mounting interface.

### 3.3 Interpretation

Several of the conditions above produce similar vibration signatures, and more than one may be
present at the same time. A single elevated vibration reading does not identify which of them
applies. Identification requires inspection, and where inspection is inconclusive, further
diagnostic measurement.

This manual does not authorize any operational action. Stopping, isolating or restarting the
machine is governed by the site procedure.
